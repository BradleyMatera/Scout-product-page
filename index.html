01 / Design principles

Not another chatbot template.

Scout is being developed around a reusable core rather than hard-coding one industry, one company, one model provider or one knowledge base into the product.

[01]Core before customer

The engine should remain functional without a customer-specific knowledge package installed.

[02]Grounded when it matters

When a request depends on business facts, Scout is designed to retrieve authoritative evidence rather than casually inventing answers.

[03]Tools are capabilities

External actions should connect through defined contracts instead of being scattered as one-off customer logic throughout the engine.

[04]Portable by design

Changing the domain should mean changing a package or configuration, not rebuilding the intelligence engine for every customer.

[05]Validate the output

Generation is not automatically treated as truth. Scout's architecture includes validation, repair and failure handling as first-class concerns.

[06]Efficient on purpose

Deterministic retrieval, routing and validation can reduce unnecessary model use and preserve compute for the tasks that actually need it.

02 / Specialization

One engine. Different jobs.

Scout's specialization layer is intended to carry the customer-specific material: facts, documents, policies, workflows, tool declarations, rules and controlled extensions.

03 / Use cases

Broad engine. Specific outcome.

Scout is intentionally broad underneath, but customers should buy a clear result. These are examples of the kinds of specialized deployments the architecture is meant to support.

Customer support

Answer approved questions from company knowledge, collect the right context, use permitted tools and escalate when Scout should not guess.

Sales and lead qualification

Understand products, answer buyer questions, qualify opportunities and connect to business systems through customer-approved capabilities.

Internal knowledge assistant

Help employees navigate procedures, documentation, systems and policies using authoritative internal information rather than general model memory.

Technical and operational support

Combine documentation retrieval, structured workflows, validation and controlled tools for environments where a confident wrong answer is expensive.

Personal or device-specific assistant

Attach the knowledge and tools for a specific environment while keeping the intelligence layer independent from that single environment.

Agent-to-agent systems

Use Scout as an orchestration and reasoning component that can communicate with other systems and agents through explicit interfaces.
