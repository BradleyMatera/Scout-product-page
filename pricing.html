02 / Transfer scope

“All of it” has to be defined.

Source code, ownership, branding, personal data, deployment accounts, and future obligations are separate things. They should not be silently bundled together under a vague promise to “buy Scout.”

01

Source

The repositories, branches, commits, documentation, tests, assets, and configuration files that are actually named in the agreement.

Defined by repository and commit
02

Rights

Permission to use, modify, operate, sublicense, or exclusively own the transferred materials must be stated explicitly.

Not implied by payment
03

Operations

The buyer supplies its own hosting, provider accounts, secrets, domains, credentials, data, and production operating process unless the agreement says otherwise.

Buyer-operated by default
04

Handoff

Any walkthrough, transition period, bug clarification, or limited assistance is listed as a finite deliverable instead of becoming indefinite support.

Bounded and written

Current technical reality.

Scout has real retrieval, session, inference, tool, validation, telemetry, and deployment work behind it. It is still a ProjectHub Recruiter Alpha implementation, not a finished customer-neutral SaaS product.

What a buyer getsAn existing engineering implementation that can be inspected and operated by a capable technical team.
What a buyer must doReview the code, configure its own environment, validate its use case, and accept responsibility for operation after transfer.
What this page does not claimThat Scout is turnkey, universally portable, production-supported for every domain, or ready for self-service customers.

03 / Process

How one acquisition is evaluated.

The process is designed to make the asset and the obligations clear before either side commits to a transfer.

01

State the intended use

Explain what you want to operate, extend, or acquire and who will own the technical work afterward.

02

Define “all”

Identify the exact repositories, rights, branding, documentation, data boundaries, and transition materials involved.

03

Inspect the asset

Review the implementation, source history, tests, deployment notes, dependencies, and known limitations.

04

Write one agreement

Record the price, rights, exclusions, payment terms, handoff, and any finite transition assistance.

05

Transfer and close

Deliver the agreed package and complete the defined handoff. No ongoing service is created unless separately agreed.

04 / Questions

Clear answers before a conversation.

This page describes the commercial shape. A final transaction would be governed by its written terms.

Is Scout being sold as a monthly service?

No. This page no longer advertises hosted plans, managed operations, or recurring service tiers.

Is there a public fixed price?

There is one transaction, but the price depends on the rights and materials being transferred. An arbitrary number would imply a scope that has not been defined.

Does paying for source code automatically transfer ownership?

No. Ownership, exclusivity, modification rights, redistribution, and permitted use must be written explicitly.

Does the buyer receive personal recruiter data?

Not by default. Personal identity, portfolio content, private credentials, and personal data are separate boundaries and would need explicit treatment.

Will Bradley provide ongoing development?

Not as part of this offer. Any limited transition help would be finite, separately listed, and agreed before transfer.

Is Scout a turnkey product for any industry?

No. The current implementation is ProjectHub Recruiter Alpha. A customer-neutral core and generalized domain packages remain productization work.
